A free numeric-evidence practice collection

Explain what your numbers mean.

Six standalone exercises for checking arithmetic, defining a measurement and separating a useful result from an unsupported claim.

Each page contains a small fictional dataset, explicit definitions, hand-calculable questions, a printable worksheet and a fully reasoned answer key. The exercises cover different reasoning problems; you can begin with whichever one resembles a measurement you need to explain.

Work from paper

  1. Read the packet and write the unit beside every number: requests, people, minutes, releases or percentage points.
  2. Try the questions with the key covered. Show your denominator, weights and assumptions. A calculator is optional.
  3. Read the worked key and correct the reasoning, not just the final number. Explain which conclusions remain unsupported.
  4. Ask a partner to use the follow-up questions, or answer them aloud yourself. Then use your own truthful work evidence for a separate interview rehearsal.

All data and organizations are fictional. These examples are not work experience, credentials or validated assessments. They cannot be used to claim real outcomes or expertise.

  • 1. Weighted averages when the work mix changes

    Explain how both groups improve while the overall average gets worse.

  • 2. A mean is not a nearest-rank percentile

    Calculate a small latency distribution and explain what a slow tail changes.

  • 3. Throughput is not backlog or lead time

    Balance a work queue, calculate required capacity and resist a misleading waiting-time shortcut.

  • 4. An early comparison is not a settled result

    Reconstruct a small comparison and identify what stopping and assignment leave unresolved.

  • 5. A scenario range is not a probability claim

    Build an effort envelope, test a capacity limit and explain what a range cannot promise.

  • 6. Overlapping contributions cannot simply be added

    Count unique releases, distinguish participation from ownership and avoid credit inflation.

Print and read offline

Open any exercise and use Print. The worksheet starts on its own page, and the answer key begins afterward. Keep the key pages aside until you finish the calculation. You can also save these static documents and open index.html locally with style.css alongside it. No JavaScript, external fonts, analytics, accounts or data collection are used.

What the lab does not establish

Correct arithmetic does not create a representative sample, causal evidence, an outcome probability or a hiring verdict. Each exercise names the particular assumptions needed for its calculations and the limits that remain. The collection is educational practice, with no professional hiring or financial claims.
